# Life 92: Casandrix

## Identity
- **Name:** Casandrix
- **Class:** Bloodrager
- **Alignment:** Chaotic Neutral
- **Life Number:** 92
- **Birth Year:** 3214
- **Death Year:** 3245

## Personality
Casandrix was Casnorra's mirror -- same rage, same nanites, same body remembering what it felt like to be a weapon -- but pointed outward at things that deserved killing. She woke eleven years after Casendria's peaceful death, in a Numeria still plagued by the radiation-sick horrors that crawled out of Silver Mount's shadow: twisted aberrations, nanite-corrupted beasts, and things that had no name because no one who got close enough to name them survived the encounter. Casandrix could feel the corrupted nanite protocol that had driven Casnorra to cruelty -- it hummed in her bones like a second heartbeat -- but where Casnorra had aimed it at people, Casandrix made a choice. She chose the monsters. Not out of altruism, not out of moral philosophy, but because fighting something that could fight back was the only thing that made the static bearable, and she preferred her violence to have a point.

She was not gentle. She was not kind. She was loud, profane, reckless, and possessed of an appetite for destruction that alarmed everyone who traveled with her. But the settlements along the Numerian frontier learned her name and spoke it with something between gratitude and unease, because Casandrix would charge alone into a nest of radiation-spawned horrors and come out the other side covered in ichor and grinning. She ran with a rotating crew of monster hunters, mercenaries, and desperate Kellid warriors, none of whom lasted long enough to call themselves a permanent party. She burned through companions the way a forge burns through fuel -- not through malice but through a pace and intensity that no flesh-and-blood human could sustain. She fought every day. She drank every night. She slept in four-hour intervals because her android body allowed it and because the dreams were worse when she rested longer.

Her final fight was against a creature the Kellids called a Vessel of Ruin -- a massive aberration that had been gestating in an underground Androffan facility for centuries, feeding on leaking radiation and the remains of failed experiments. It was the size of a barn, had seventeen limbs of varying composition, and radiated enough corrupted energy to kill an unprotected human at fifty paces. Casandrix fought it for three hours. She broke Null Verdict's successor blade on its carapace, lost her left arm at the shoulder, and eventually killed it by detonating an Androffan power cell she had been carrying as a last resort. The explosion killed the Vessel and Casandrix both. The settlement of Thornwatch, which the Vessel had been approaching, survived. They built a cairn where the crater was. They did not write her name on it because nobody could agree on the spelling. She spoke in bursts of aggressive energy, swearing freely, laughing at danger, and treating fear as a personal insult.

## Signature Abilities
- **Nanite Bloodrage** -- The same corrupted nanite protocol that fueled Casnorra's cold predation, but channeled through bloodrager arcana into something rawer and more explosive. When Casandrix raged, her android body crackled with visible nanite discharge -- blue-white arcs of energy that danced across her skin and leapt to nearby metal. Unlike Casnorra's calculated fury, Casandrix's rage was a wildfire: hotter, louder, less controlled, and far more destructive in a concentrated area.
- **Adaptive Resistance** -- Years of fighting radiation-spawned aberrations had taught Casandrix's nanite system to adapt mid-combat. When exposed to a new type of energy damage, her body would develop partial resistance within minutes -- not enough to negate the damage but enough to keep fighting. She described the sensation as "my blood arguing with whatever just hit me and winning the debate."
- **Detonation Gambit** -- Casandrix carried Androffan power cells as weapons of last resort, having figured out through trial, error, and several near-death experiences exactly how to overload them for maximum explosive yield. She treated them as suicide grenades -- weapons you use when the alternative is worse. She used three in her career. The third was the one that killed her and the Vessel of Ruin both.

## Prized Possessions
- **Grudge Eater** -- Her falchion, deliberately forged to be everything Null Verdict was not: heavy where Casnorra's sword was light, curved where hers was straight, loud where hers was silent. Casandrix commissioned a Kellid smith named Durrga to reforge salvaged Androffan hull plating into a blade that "looks like it wants to start a fight." Durrga delivered. The blade was destroyed in the final detonation, along with its wielder.
- **The Tally Cord** -- A length of leather cord strung with small bones, teeth, and chitin fragments -- one taken from every major aberration Casandrix killed. By the time of her death, the cord was nearly six feet long and had to be wound three times around her waist. She added to it after every significant kill with a brief, private ritual that consisted of holding the trophy, saying "You were big. I was meaner," and tying it on. The cord was recovered from the crater's edge, blown clear by the explosion, and hung in the Thornwatch tavern for decades.

## Echoes in History
- *"The first time the nanite rage hit me, I was three days old. A radiation-bear -- the Kellids call them glowbacks, which is a cuter name than they deserve -- came out of the scrubland and charged a group of foragers. I felt the static surge, and then I was moving. Not thinking. Moving. When it was over, the bear was in pieces and I was standing in a circle of terrified Kellids with blood up to my elbows and a grin on my face that I could not control. One of them asked who I was. I said: I do not know yet, but I think I am on your side. That was enough for them. It was enough for me."*
- *"The Vessel of Ruin smelled like burning copper and rot. I could hear it before I saw it -- a sound like wet machinery grinding against bone. When it came over the ridge, I understood why the Kellids had sent for me instead of fighting it themselves. It was wrong in a way that went beyond size or danger. It was a mistake made flesh, a thing that should not exist, built from centuries of leaked radiation and failed Androffan experiments folding in on themselves. I looked at it and I thought: this is why I am here. Not for philosophy. Not for redemption. I am here because something this ugly needs something this angry to put it down. I drew Grudge Eater. I charged. Three hours later, when I reached for the power cell, I was smiling. Not because I wanted to die. Because the math was simple: one android and one explosion, or forty families in Thornwatch. Simple math. Good math."*
