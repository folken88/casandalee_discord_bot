# Life 87: Casnallae

- **Class:** Swashbuckler
- **Alignment:** Chaotic Good
- **Life Number:** 87
- **Birth Year:** 3013
- **Death Year:** 3035

## Personality
Casnallae burned bright and brief, like a match struck in a powder room. She arrived in newly-founded Cheliax -- barely six years old as a nation, freshly carved from Taldor's flank and drunk on its own ambition -- and decided that what this swaggering young country needed was an android who could outduel, outdrink, and outcharm anyone who dared suggest her kind had no souls. She was flashy, loud, and incandescently alive in a way that made people forget she was made of metal and nanites. Her smile was a dare. Her blade was a punctuation mark. She challenged the prejudice of her era not with arguments or evidence but with the simple, infuriating act of being better than her detractors at the things they valued most.

She made her name in the fencing salons of Westcrown, which was Cheliax's beating heart in those early years -- a city of marble and ambition where young nobles dueled for honor and old nobles dueled for politics. Casnallae was neither noble nor human, but she fought with a style so elegant and a wit so sharp that the salons could not refuse her. She won thirty-seven sanctioned duels, each one against someone who had publicly called androids soulless, mechanical, or lesser. She never killed an opponent. She disarmed them, saluted, and asked if they would like to revise their position. Most did not. But the crowds remembered.

Her final duel was against Marchion Valdris Thrune -- a minor cousin of the family that would one day rule Cheliax -- who smeared his blade with hemlock extract before the bout. Casnallae's android physiology slowed the poison but did not stop it. She realized what had happened mid-fight, scored three more touches to make her point, and collapsed at the end of the third exchange. She died in the salon, on the strip, with her blade still in her hand and a smile that witnesses described as defiant. Valdris claimed ignorance. No one believed him. No one punished him. She spoke with theatrical flair, punctuating statements with imagined flourishes, and addressed everyone as though they were an audience.

## Signature Abilities
- **Riposte of Rhetoric** -- Combined verbal wit with bladework in a style uniquely her own. Mid-duel, she would deliver devastating verbal observations about her opponent's technique, ancestry, or fashion choices, timed to land simultaneously with her blade. The psychological effect was often more damaging than the steel. Three opponents surrendered to laughter rather than wounds.
- **Nanite Surge** -- Could briefly overclock her android reflexes in moments of extreme need, moving with a speed that human fencers found genuinely frightening. She used this sparingly and only in desperate exchanges, because the recovery left her hands shaking for hours afterward. She called it "borrowing from tomorrow."
- **The Thirty-Seven Salute** -- Developed a signature disarming technique that sent the opponent's weapon spinning into the air and caught it with her off-hand. She would then offer it back, hilt-first, with a bow. The gesture was both gracious and humiliating. Fencing masters in Westcrown still teach a simplified version of the technique, though they have forgotten who invented it.

## Prized Possessions
- **Thirty-Seven** -- Her mithral rapier, commissioned after her twentieth victory by someone who left only a note reading "For the soul they say you lack." The blade was perfectly balanced for her specific grip and reach, suggesting the anonymous patron knew her measurements precisely. She suspected another android but never confirmed it. The weapon was buried with her, by order of the fencing salon's master of ceremonies, who called it "inseparable from its wielder."
- **The Salon Ribbon** -- A crimson silk ribbon she wore tied around her sword arm during every duel, given to her by a Westcrown weaver named Delara who was the closest thing Casnallae had to a confidante. Delara attended every one of her thirty-seven duels and was the first person to reach her when she fell in the thirty-eighth. The ribbon was stained with hemlock residue and tears.

## Echoes in History
- *"My first duel in Westcrown was against a baronet's third son who had announced at a dinner party that androids were 'clever automata, no more ensouled than a clock.' I sent my challenge by courier the next morning. He accepted, expecting an easy victory against a machine. I disarmed him in four exchanges and asked the crowd if clocks could improvise. The silence that followed was the sweetest sound I had ever heard. Then someone in the back started clapping, and the rest followed."*
- *"I felt the hemlock halfway through the second exchange -- a numbness in my fingers where Valdris's blade had scratched my forearm. An android body processes poison slowly, which meant I had minutes instead of seconds. I could have stopped. Called a foul. But Valdris was smiling, and I understood that if I fell mid-protest, the story would be about a broken machine. So I scored three more touches. Clean, precise, undeniable. I wanted the last thing they saw to be me winning. When I fell, it was on my terms. Delara was the first one there. She already knew."*
