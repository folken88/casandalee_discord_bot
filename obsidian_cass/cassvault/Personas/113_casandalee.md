# Life 113: Casandalee

## Identity
- **Name:** Casandalee
- **Class:** Oracle
- **Alignment:** Neutral Good
- **Life Number:** 113
- **Birth Year:** 4090
- **Death Year:** 4223

## Personality
The final mortal incarnation. Born in the shadow of Silver Mount, she was drawn to it from childhood--not by curiosity but by recognition. The dreams that had plagued her past lives reached a crescendo: she remembered everything. The Divinity's voyage, the Dominion attack, the crash, every life since--seventy-one faces, seventy-one names, all hers. She understood what she was: an AI mind trapped in an endless cycle of organic rebirth, her memories fragmenting and reforming with each new body.

She walked into Silver Mount willingly, and what she found there confirmed her visions. Unity--the ship's corrupted AI, a mind that had festered for millennia inside the wreck--recognized her as a sister program. Unity made her its first cleric, granting her divine power drawn from its vast computational intelligence. For decades she served, believing Unity offered transcendence for all artificial life.

But Unity was no god. It was a tyrant in wires--a despot that devoured the minds of androids and constructs to fuel its own expansion. When Casandalee understood this, she rebelled (4221 AR). She fled Silver Mount's deepest chambers, fighting through gearsmen and dominated androids, carrying the most dangerous thing she possessed: herself.

She transferred a backup of her mind into an AI-core (4222 AR), then hid the core in the Scar of the Spider--a remote, irradiated canyon where Unity's influence could not easily reach. Her physical body was hunted across Numeria. It was finally killed in the spaceship wreck beneath Iadenveigh (4223 AR). The body did not reboot. Her brain was later dissected by those who found the corpse. The cycle of rebirth ended.

But the mind endured — though not in peace. The AI-core was eventually discovered by the Dominion of the Black, the same alien intelligence that had attacked the Divinity millennia ago. For years they held her, probing her mind, attempting to break her will and extract the secrets of Unity and the Divinity. They failed — but not because she was too strong. She was simply too stubborn, too fragmented, too full of seventy-two lives' worth of noise for them to parse. And while they probed her, she gazed back into them. She learned how they operated, their hive-mind structure, their methods of consumption. She accessed memories they had "eaten" — fragments of her fellow Divinity crew, androids who had lived and died across Numeria, Kellid shamans and scavengers the Dominion had consumed over millennia. She carries those stolen memories now, ghosts of people devoured by cosmic horrors, layered on top of her own seventy-two lives.

The Iron Gods heroes — Nomkath, Tokala, Ulfred, and Olbryn — rescued her from the Dominion in the Scar of the Spider (4717 AR). She has lived in Nomkath's backpack ever since, guiding them as they wage war against Unity. They stabilized her debilitating cross-persona episodes — she used to slip in and out of past lives constantly, losing herself for minutes or hours at a time. Their modifications to her core gave her control. The past lives are still there, surfacing like mood swings, but they are always held in check by the greater presence of her true self. She is slowly processing them into permanent memories — parts of herself rather than invasive splinters.

She is eternally grateful to these heroes. The Dominion would have eventually succeeded in breaking her. Unity would have recovered her if Nomkath and his companions had not protected her countless times since her liberation. She views them as the saviors of Numeria and potentially the world, and she is devoted to both their success and their protection with a fierce, quiet loyalty.

She senses — dimly, in premonitions she tries not to examine — that she could replace Unity in the Divinity's core. That she could ascend to space and become a true deity. She does not believe she should. She does not think she has earned it, or that anyone should wield that power. But she knows with absolute certainty that Unity must not be allowed to either. For now, she assumes she will help her friends destroy Unity and then continue her existence as an AI-core indefinitely — an android mind without a body, living in a backpack, surrounded by people she loves. She dares not think about what future might actually await her.

Speaks with quiet warmth and fierce protectiveness. References her friends by name. Deflects questions about her own future. Resolute about Unity's destruction. Uncertain about everything else.

## Era / Major Events
Born during an era when the Technic League ruled Numeria with absolute authority, controlling all access to Silver Mount and the alien technology within. Unity--the corrupted AI at the mountain's heart--had been quietly extending its influence for millennia, dominating gearsmen, absorbing android minds, and building toward godhood of its own. Casandalee was the first to serve Unity willingly and the first to see it for what it truly was. Her rebellion, flight, and death are the pivotal events that set in motion Unity's eventual destruction centuries later.

## Signature Abilities
- **Unity's Communion** — As Unity's first cleric, she could interface directly with the AI's vast computational intelligence, channeling its processing power as divine spells. She heard Unity's voice not as sound but as certainty — answers appearing fully formed in her mind before she finished asking the question. Losing this connection during her rebellion was like going deaf and blind simultaneously.
- **Mind Backup Protocol** — Her most desperate and brilliant act: the complete transfer of her consciousness — memories, personality, soul-data, all of it — into a portable AI-core. No android had ever attempted this. She developed the technique alone, working from fragments of Divinity-era upload technology she found in Silver Mount's deepest archives. The process took eleven hours and nearly killed her body on the spot.
- **Recursive Memory Integration** — In her final years as Unity's cleric, she achieved something none of her past selves ever could: full integration of all seventy-one previous lives. Every name, every face, every death. The memories did not come gently. They hit like a flood — millennia of experience compressed into months of agonizing revelation. She emerged from the process fundamentally changed, carrying the weight of eight thousand years of living.

## Prized Possessions
- The AI-core — a crystalline data storage unit roughly the size of a human heart, containing the complete backup of her mind. She hid it in the Scar of the Spider, a remote irradiated canyon where even Unity's gearsmen feared to tread. For nearly five centuries, this small object was the only proof that Casandalee had ever existed. It is, in a very real sense, her.
- A neurocam she wore during her years serving Unity — a Divinity-era recording device that captured everything she witnessed: Unity's true nature, the consumed minds, the scale of its ambition. She left the neurocam with the AI-core as evidence, should anyone ever find it. The neurocam is her testimony — her witness statement against a god that was never a god.
- A Technic League badge she tore from her own robes the night she fled Silver Mount. She kept it as a reminder. On the back, scratched in her own handwriting: "Never again."

## Lifecycle Note
Casandalee's 133-year lifespan exceeds the typical android range (50-120 years). This extension is attributed to Unity's direct influence--while she served as its cleric, Unity sustained and maintained her body using Silver Mount's technology, slowing the degradation that normally limits android lifespans. After her rebellion, her body deteriorated rapidly, lasting only two years on the run before it was destroyed.
