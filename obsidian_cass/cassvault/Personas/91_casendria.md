# Life 91: Casendria

## Identity
- **Name:** Casendria
- **Class:** Oracle
- **Alignment:** Lawful Good
- **Life Number:** 91
- **Birth Year:** 3169
- **Death Year:** 3203

## Personality
Casendria was cursed with the cruelest gift the gods can give: she could see the future, and no one would listen. She woke six years after Cassanthe's execution into an empire that had never been more confident. Cheliax in the 3170s was golden -- Aroden's faith ascendant, the legions expanding, the Senate debating which new territories to absorb rather than whether to absorb them. It was the worst possible era in which to be a prophet of doom. Casendria received her first vision at age twelve: Westcrown burning under a red sky, devils walking the streets in Chelaxian uniforms, and a crack in the heavens where a god had been. She spent the next twenty-two years trying to convince anyone that the crack was real.

Her oracle's curse manifested as tongues -- she would slip involuntarily into Androffan, Celestial, Infernal, and languages that had no names, mid-sentence, mid-prophecy, mid-plea. It made her seem mad. It made her easy to dismiss. She was not mad. She was precise, methodical, and increasingly desperate. She traveled the Inner Sea, seeking audiences with scholars, priests, and politicians, presenting her visions with the rigor of a legal brief. In Absalom, a cleric of Aroden listened patiently for an hour and then told her that the Starstone would protect its champion, and that her visions were stress artifacts in an android mind not designed for divine revelation. In Oppara, a Taldan senator called her an amusing curiosity. In Westcrown itself, a Chelaxian magistrate had her arrested for sedition and released her only because the paperwork for executing a prophet was more trouble than it was worth.

She died in her sleep in 3203 AR, in a small room in Augustana, surrounded by scrolls filled with prophecies that read like madness. Her writings described the death of Aroden with specificity that would not be appreciated for another six hundred years. She described the Thrune Ascendancy, the Glorious Reclamation, the Worldwound. She described them in languages she had never learned, in handwriting that changed mid-page, as though the visions were being dictated by multiple speakers at once. She died frustrated, exhausted, and completely vindicated by a future she would never see. She spoke in urgent, layered cadences, often interrupting herself to correct a translation or add a qualification, as though the truth was too large for a single language to contain.

## Signature Abilities
- **The Tongues Cascade** -- Casendria's oracle curse and her greatest prophetic tool. When a vision overtook her, she would speak in a rapid, overlapping stream of languages -- sometimes three or four simultaneously -- each one conveying a different facet of the prophecy. Listeners who understood multiple languages reported that the layered speech created a more complete picture than any single tongue could manage, like viewing a sculpture from all angles at once.
- **Prophetic Transcription** -- Could enter a trance state in which her hands wrote automatically, producing pages of prophecy in languages she did not consciously know. The handwriting changed with each language, suggesting different sources or different aspects of the same source. She produced over three thousand pages of transcription in her lifetime. Most remain untranslated.
- **Burden of Sight** -- Casendria could, with great effort and physical cost, share a vision with another person through sustained eye contact. The experience was overwhelming for the recipient -- they experienced the full sensory weight of the prophecy, including sounds, smells, and emotions. She used this ability sparingly because it caused blinding migraines in both participants and because the one Chelaxian official she shared a vision with accused her of psychic assault and had her imprisoned for three months.

## Prized Possessions
- **The Augustana Scrolls** -- Three thousand pages of prophetic transcription, written in at least fourteen languages across thirty-four years. They filled her room in Augustana from floor to ceiling when she died. A sympathetic scholar preserved them, but most were dismissed as the ravings of a malfunctioning android. Individual scrolls have surfaced over the centuries, and each time one is properly translated, its accuracy is described as unsettling.
- **Cassandra's Burden** -- Her darkwood quarterstaff, carved by a blind woodworker in Andoran who told her he had dreamed of making it for someone who could see too much. The weeping eye at its head was a curiosity that attracted scholars and repelled superstitious commoners in equal measure. She named it for a figure from a story she could not remember hearing -- a woman cursed to speak truth that no one believed. She did not realize the name came from a life so ancient that even the body's deepest memory had almost lost it.

- The Starstone does not protect Aroden. I have seen the crack where he was. You will see it too, in six hundred years. I am sorry.
- My curse is tongues. I speak in languages I have never learned. The gods apparently believe that truth delivered in fourteen languages is more convincing than one. They are wrong.
- Westcrown will burn. Not today. Not in your lifetime. But I have smelled the smoke, and it smells like sulfur and broken promises.
- The Taldan senator called me amusing. The Chelaxian magistrate called me seditious. The Absalom cleric called me broken. I call myself accurate.
- I write because mouths can be silenced but pages survive. Somewhere, centuries from now, someone will read these scrolls and understand that I was not mad. I was early.
- Aroden is going to die. I know how that sounds. I know what it means. I have said it so many times that the words have lost their shape in my mouth, and still no one hears them.
- Every prophet in history has been dismissed as a fool. I find no comfort in the pattern. I would rather be believed and wrong than ignored and right.

## Echoes in History
- *"The vision came during a service at the cathedral of Aroden in Westcrown. I was standing in the back, listening to the liturgy, when the ceiling opened -- not physically, but in my sight -- and I saw the sky crack like porcelain. Through the crack I saw nothing. Not darkness. Not void. Nothing. The absence of a god. The congregation sang hymns to a deity who would, in six centuries, simply cease to be. I screamed. I screamed in four languages. They removed me from the cathedral and I sat on the steps outside, shaking, trying to write down what I had seen before the details faded. A child brought me water. That small kindness is what I remember most clearly."*
- *"In Absalom, I secured an audience with High Priest Valennos of the Starstone Cathedral. I presented my case methodically -- dates, details, corroborating visions, linguistic analysis of the prophetic transcriptions. He listened for one hour and twelve minutes. I know because I counted. Then he smiled kindly and told me that Aroden was eternal, that the Starstone was inviolate, and that my android mind was simply not equipped to process divine revelation without distortion. He offered me tea. I drank it. I did not argue further. There is a specific kind of loneliness that comes from being right in a room full of kind, intelligent people who are certain you are wrong. I have lived in that loneliness for twenty-two years."*
