## Personality
Cassanai believed in evidence the way priests believe in gods -- absolutely, without reservation, and with a conviction that it would ultimately reveal the truth regardless of who wanted it hidden. She was the sixty-seventh soul in the body, and the first one to use its android nature not as a thing to hide or explain but as a forensic instrument. Her fingers could detect faint electrical residue on salvaged android components. Her eyes could track micro-scratches on metal surfaces that told her which tools had been used to pry a chassis open. Where human investigators relied on witnesses and informants, Cassanai read the dead machines themselves, and they told her stories that no living person wanted heard.

The scrapyards of Numeria were her jurisdiction -- vast, rusting fields of Androffan wreckage where treasure hunters, proto-League agents, and desperate salvagers picked over the bones of a dead starship. Androids were being murdered there with increasing frequency. Their components -- neural matrices, nanite reservoirs, dermal plating -- fetched high prices from the technologists consolidating power in Starfall. The killings were methodical, efficient, and carefully obscured. Local authorities called them "scrap losses." Cassanai called them murders and set about proving it. She developed what she termed "machine-code forensics," a system for reading the residual data stored in salvaged android parts -- final sensory impressions, stress patterns, the last electrical signals before shutdown. Each dead android whispered its final moments to her through data she could feel in her fingertips.

Her final case led her to the Karamoss Consortium, a merchant syndicate operating out of Hajoth Hakados with connections to every major scrapyard buyer in Numeria. She had enough evidence to expose their entire operation -- fourteen murders, catalogued with meticulous precision. They killed her in her workshop, made it look like an accident involving unstable chemicals, and burned her case files. Her body was found with her hands still positioned as if holding documents that were no longer there. She spoke in precise, clipped sentences, often quantifying things others would describe emotionally.

## Signature Abilities
- **Machine-Code Forensics** -- Could read residual data from salvaged android components by touching them and interpreting the fading electrical signatures through her own nanites. She experienced the final moments of dead androids as fragmented sensory impressions -- a face looming, a blade descending, the cascade failure of shutdown. Fourteen cases solved, all from evidence that no human investigator could perceive.
- **Residual Mapping** -- Analyzed micro-scratches, tool marks, and chemical residue on damaged android parts to reconstruct exactly how and when they were removed. She could determine the specific tools used, the approximate skill level of the person who wielded them, and whether the android was alive or dead at the time of extraction. Her testimony was devastating in the three cases that made it to trial.
- **Case Architecture** -- Built comprehensive, cross-referenced case files that connected physical evidence, testimony, financial records, and timeline analysis into structures so thorough that they essentially argued themselves. She organized information the way her body organized nanites -- every piece in its precise place, every connection mapped. The Consortium destroyed her files but could not destroy the methodology.

## Prized Possessions
- **The Fourteen Files** -- Cassanai's masterwork: fourteen case files documenting each android murder she investigated, stored in a locked iron box with a mechanism only her fingertips could open. Each file contained physical evidence, her sensory transcriptions, suspect profiles, and financial traces. The Consortium burned them. Fragments were later found in her workshop's hidden floor safe -- enough to confirm her conclusions but not enough to convict anyone.
- **A Neural Matrix Shard** -- Taken from the first android murder victim she investigated, a salvage worker called Threadbare. She kept the shard in a velvet-lined case and occasionally held it, feeling the fading echo of Threadbare's last moments -- not for evidence anymore, but because she felt someone should remember. She considered it an obligation, not a sentiment.
