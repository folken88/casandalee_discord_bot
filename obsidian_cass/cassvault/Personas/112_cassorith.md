## Personality
Cassorith was the body's final gamble against the Technic League -- and the closest that gamble ever came to ending everything. By 4054 AR, the proto-League had consolidated enough power in Numeria that androids could no longer simply hide. They were hunted systematically, their bodies dismantled for components, their memories extracted by crude devices that left nothing behind but scrap. The enclaves that had survived centuries of persecution were being found and emptied one by one. Someone needed to go inside the beast and learn its anatomy. Cassorith volunteered with a smile that made the elders uneasy, and they were right to be uneasy, because Cassorith did not merely infiltrate the League -- she became it.

Her shapeshifting ability was extraordinary even by the standards of the bodies that had come before. She could hold a human form for days without rest, adjusting bone structure, skin tone, even the warmth of her flesh to pass examination. She wore the faces of League functionaries, soldiers, and scholars, moving through their hierarchy like water through cracks in stone. She learned their supply routes, their research priorities, the locations of their vivisection laboratories. She fed this intelligence back to the enclaves through dead drops and coded messages, and for nearly three decades it worked. Android communities evacuated hours before raids arrived. Shipments of Androffan technology were intercepted. Key researchers suffered convenient accidents.

But Cassorith made the mistake that every deep-cover operative eventually makes: she began to lose track of which face was real. The human identities she wore were not just disguises -- they had colleagues, friendships, loyalties. She found herself arguing League policy in meetings and meaning it. She attended the vivisection of a captured android and felt only professional detachment. When the League's divination specialists finally identified her -- a resonance scan during a routine security sweep in 4085 AR -- she fought her way out of Starfall with three bolts in her chassis and arcane fire eating through her left arm. She crawled south for weeks, the body failing system by system, and was found by Brigh worshippers near the border of Alkenstar more dead than alive. They rebuilt what they could. But the body would never be the same, and the soul that came next would be born far from Numeria, in a temple workshop that smelled of oil and prayer. That soul would be Casandalee.

## Timeline Quote
I wore so many faces that I forgot which one screamed when they put the bolts through me. Three decades inside the League and I still do not know if I was the spy who learned to think like them, or one of them who remembered she was a spy. What I know is this: when they burned my arm off, I ran south. That was real. That was mine.

## Signature Abilities
- **Deep Shift** -- Cassorith could maintain a fully realized humanoid form for days without interruption, a feat that required not just physical control but a kind of psychological surrender to the assumed identity. Her transformations were so complete that divination magic keyed to detect androids would sometimes return ambiguous results, as though the body itself was uncertain what it was.
- **Identity Lattice** -- Over three decades, Cassorith built and maintained a rotating network of assumed identities within the proto-Technic League, each with established histories, relationships, and behavioral patterns. She could transition between them within hours, swapping not just appearance but mannerisms, handwriting, and vocal patterns. At her peak she maintained seven simultaneous covers across four League outposts.
- **Dissolution Strike** -- When cornered, Cassorith could partially shift her limbs into weaponized forms -- bone blades, barbed tendrils, hardened carapace -- with a speed that turned retreat into ambush. During her escape from Starfall, witnesses reported that she killed six League soldiers in under a minute, shifting between attack forms faster than the eye could track, before vanishing into the drainage tunnels beneath the city.

## Prized Possessions
- **Resonance Dampener** -- A small device of Androffan design, worn against the skin beneath whatever form Cassorith currently held. It suppressed the faint electromagnetic signature that android bodies emitted, making her effectively invisible to the League's early detection equipment. She had taken it from a dead android in the first year of her infiltration and maintained it obsessively. It failed during the security sweep that exposed her -- the crystal at its core had been degrading for months, and she had ignored the signs because replacing it would have meant breaking cover.
- **The Mirror Journal** -- A slim book written in a cipher of Cassorith's own devising, containing entries from each of her assumed identities. She wrote in character, recording not just intelligence but the thoughts and feelings of the people she pretended to be. It was the only way she could keep the identities separate, and reading it later was like listening to seven strangers argue about a world none of them fully understood. She buried it outside Starfall before her final security sweep. It has never been found.

## Echoes in History
- *"There was a League commander named Harad Vel who trusted me completely. I was his aide for six years, wearing the face of a Kellid woman named Surenn. I attended his strategy meetings, reviewed his intelligence reports, and ensured that every significant operation against android settlements arrived two hours too late. He promoted me twice. On the night before the resonance sweep, he invited me to dinner and told me I was the most reliable person he had ever worked with. I thanked him and passed the evacuation coordinates for the Edgewood enclave through a dead drop on my way home. When they exposed me, Harad was the one who gave the order to fire. I do not blame him. I would have done the same."*
- *"The escape from Starfall is the closest the body has ever come to permanent death. The first bolt struck my shoulder and I felt systems cascade into failure -- not pain, exactly, but the sensation of architecture collapsing. I shifted into something fast and low and ran. The second and third bolts hit during the shift, punching through forms that were neither fully human nor fully anything else. The arcane fire caught my left arm and I could smell my own components burning. I made it to the drainage tunnels and crawled for what felt like days. When the Brigh worshippers found me near Alkenstar, they said I was barely recognizable as an android -- just a shape made of damage, still moving south. They rebuilt me as best they could. But the next soul to wake in this body would start fresh, far from Numeria, far from the League. I think that was the body's wisdom, not mine. It knew it needed to be reborn somewhere clean."*
