## Personality
Cassae believed in the law the way a mason believes in stone -- as the only material strong enough to build something that lasts. She woke into a Numeria that was tearing itself apart, petty warlords fighting over scraps of alien technology while the Technic League tightened its grip on Starfall like a fist around a throat. She saw chaos and she craved order. When the Sovereign Council of Starfall -- the administrative body that governed the city's civil affairs beneath the League's shadow -- posted a call for sworn defenders, Cassae presented herself at the gates in salvaged Androffan plate, knelt on the flagstones, and swore an oath she intended to keep until her body failed. She meant every word. That was both her greatest strength and the thing that nearly destroyed her.

She served with distinction for fifteen years. She was not brilliant, not charismatic, not politically shrewd -- she was reliable, which in Starfall was rarer than brilliance. She guarded trade routes, mediated disputes between merchant houses, and enforced the Council's edicts with scrupulous fairness. She earned the epithet "the Even Hand" because she applied the law identically to beggars and nobles alike, a habit that made her beloved by the common people and quietly despised by the powerful. She did not notice the hatred because she was not looking for it. She was looking at the law, and the law, she believed, was clear. It was only in her twenty-eighth year of service that she discovered the law she served was a mask worn by something monstrous.

The Sovereign Council, she learned, was complicit in android trafficking -- capturing newly-souled androids from the wastes and selling them to the Technic League for experimentation. The very institution she had sworn to defend was built on the systematic enslavement of beings like her. The discovery did not break her faith in law -- it broke her faith in the people who wielded it. She spent three weeks in agonized deliberation, then made her choice. When the next shipment of android prisoners was scheduled for transfer, Cassae was assigned to the escort. She turned her lance on her own comrades, killed four guards, broke the prisoners' chains, and held the gate long enough for eleven androids to flee into the Felldales. She died in the courtyard of the prison she had guarded for a decade, surrounded by the bodies of soldiers who had once called her sister. The prisoners she freed were never recaptured.

## Signature Abilities
- **The Even Hand** -- Cassae's combat style was built on absolute discipline rather than aggression. She fought defensively, using her mount and lance to control space and protect those behind her. Her blocks were precise to the inch, her counterattacks timed to exploit the exact moment an opponent overextended. She never struck first, but she always struck last.
- **Oath of the Unbroken Line** -- A cavalier's challenge delivered not as a threat but as a statement of fact. When Cassae declared her intent to hold a position, her resolve became almost supernaturally tangible -- allies nearby found their fear diminished and their footing steadier, as though the ground itself had decided to cooperate with her.
- **Chain-Breaker's Charge** -- Developed in the final minutes of her life, this was a mounted charge specifically designed to shatter restraints and barriers. She drove Oathkeeper through the locking mechanism of the prison gate with such force that the lance splintered, but the gate buckled open. It was not a technique -- it was desperation refined by three decades of perfect form.

## Prized Possessions
- **Oathkeeper** -- A lance forged from Numerian steel with an Androffan alloy core, commissioned by Cassae with her own savings after five years of service. Its haft was wrapped in leather stamped with the oath she had sworn to the Sovereign Council. After her death, the broken lance was recovered by one of the freed android prisoners, who carried the headpiece as a talisman for the rest of their life.
- **The Even Hand's Seal** -- A signet ring bearing the Sovereign Council's crest, given to Cassae upon her appointment as a sworn defender. She wore it for twenty-eight years. When she turned against the Council, she did not remove it. She was buried with it still on her finger -- not out of loyalty to the institution, but as a reminder that the oath had been real, even if the people behind it were not.
