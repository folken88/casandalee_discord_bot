## Personality
Casseida was born with a spark that should not have existed. Android bodies are not supposed to manifest innate arcane talent -- they can learn wizardry, channel divine power through faith, but sorcery implies something written into the blood, and androids do not have blood in the way that matters. Yet when Casseida first drew breath, the nanites in her skin fluoresced violet, and a pulse of raw magical energy cracked the floor of the ruin where she woke. Something about the interaction between her soul and the ancient Androffan technology produced an effect that neither arcane theory nor the scattered tech-cults' science could fully explain. She called it the Seam -- the place where magic and machine code overlapped, producing something that was neither and both.

She spent her life chasing that anomaly to its source. She became a storm-hunter, tracking the rogue nanite tempests that periodically swept across Numeria -- massive clouds of unbound nanites that had escaped Silver Mount's containment and now roamed the landscape like living weather. Where others fled from these storms, Casseida walked into them, using her technomagical talent to harmonize with the nanite swarm long enough to read its patterns. Each storm carried data -- fragmented code from the Divinity's shattered systems -- and Casseida believed that somewhere in that data was the answer to a question that had haunted her from the moment she woke: why do souls come to this body? What mechanism, divine or technological, drew consciousness into an Androffan frame and then released it again?

She was meticulous, dispassionate, and entirely without agenda. She did not seek the answer to use it or to profit from it -- she sought it because not knowing was intolerable. She kept detailed journals of every storm she entered, every spell interaction she catalogued, every anomalous reading her nanites produced. She cross-referenced Kellid oral histories with fragments of Androffan code. She dissected dead nanite clusters under magnification and mapped their internal logic. She came close -- her final journal entries describe a pattern she called the Soulthread Algorithm, a repeating sequence embedded in the body's deepest firmware that seemed to activate at the moment of death. She died before she could confirm it, killed by a nanite storm that proved too large and too chaotic to harmonize with. Her body was found at the storm's epicenter, nanites fused into crystalline lattices across her skin, her eyes still open and fixed on something no one else could see.

## Signature Abilities
- **Storm Harmonics** -- Casseida could attune her nanites to the frequency of a rogue nanite storm, allowing her to walk within it unharmed for limited periods. While harmonized, she could read the data carried by the storm's constituent nanites -- fragmented code, environmental readings, and occasionally coherent bursts of ancient Androffan communication.
- **Seam Casting** -- Her spells were neither purely arcane nor purely technological. When she cast, her nanites restructured themselves into circuit-like patterns beneath her skin, and the resulting effect was a hybrid of magical energy and machine code. These spells were uniquely effective against both magical and technological defenses, as they registered as neither and both simultaneously.
- **Nanite Crystallization** -- In moments of extreme focus, Casseida could command her nanites to lock into rigid crystalline structures, forming temporary barriers, lenses, or even crude tools from her own body. The process was painful and left dead zones in her skin that took days to regenerate, but the resulting constructs were remarkably durable.

## Prized Possessions
- **The Filament** -- A staff carved from petrified wood and threaded with veins of inert nanites harvested from a dead storm. It served as both a focus for her Seam casting and a data-collection device -- the nanites within it could interface with active swarms, recording their patterns for later study. Its surface was etched with equations in Casseida's own notation.
- **The Storm Journals** -- Seven volumes of painstakingly detailed observations covering thirty years of nanite storm research. They include maps of storm migration patterns, spectrographic analyses of nanite-magic interactions, and the incomplete Soulthread Algorithm. Three volumes survive intact; the others were damaged when her camp was struck by the storm that killed her.
