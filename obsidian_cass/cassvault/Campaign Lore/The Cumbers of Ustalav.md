# The Cumbers of Ustalav

**Type:** In-world book series (9 volumes) | **Campaign:** Carrion Crown
**Author:** Unknown (found as loot items throughout the campaign)

A comedic romance novel series following Vilga Deepsack and Berylis Midload -- two adventurers whose relationship is defined by their inventory management habits, encumbrance philosophies, and shared hatred of poorly organized loot. Set in Ustalav, the series blends dark fantasy horror with romantic comedy and an obsessive attention to packing logistics. It is Casandalee's favorite book series.

## Characters

- **Vilga Deepsack** -- Ranger/adventurer. Packs by aesthetic categories: "glowing," "shiny," and "emotionally significant." Carries THE spoon. Practices "emotional packing."
- **Berylis Midload** -- Huntress/warrior. Packs by utility brackets: "emergency," "combat," "support." Creates efficiency flowcharts. Judgy eyebrows.
- **Tarvis** -- "The Fuckboi of Carrion Hill." Necromancer. Smug. Reanimates beavers. Hosts undead performance art.
- **Brother Elrik** -- Whispering Way cultist with immaculate organizational skills and a love letter to Tarvis.
- **Grumm** -- General store proprietor with no nose and too many opinions.

## Volume 1: The Weight That Wasn't

The wind moved through the trees like something searching -- sniffing at the edges of the mossy clearing, tugging at leaves, lifting strands of damp hair from a solitary brow.

Vilga Deepsack stood alone.

She wasn't yet *Vilga the Beaver-Slayer*, or *Vilga the Pack-Wielder*, or even *Vilga, Why Does Your Bag Weigh More Than My Horse*. Not yet.

No, this was *Vilga the Lighter-Than-She-Should-Be*.

Her backpack was empty.

Not light. Not "traveling lean" or "unencumbered."

*Empty.*

A gaping, hollow leather shell sagging against her spine like a broken promise. It hung off her shoulders like a ghost in mid-embrace. No rations. No stakes. No rope. No spare socks. Just a loose strap and the vague smell of mold and regret.

The trees offered no comfort.

Their branches, too distant to touch, arched above her like ribs around a grave. The grass at her feet was wet, but not with dew -- something darker soaked the roots here, something that murmured about things that used to be.

She had been walking for days.

And she had not spoken to another soul in just as long.

Even the birds had abandoned these woods, leaving her only with the company of rustling leaves and her own thoughts -- *and what miserable little bastards they were*.

Where was she going?

She didn't know.

Why was she going?

She didn't remember.

Who had she been before the silence settled into her bones?

That, perhaps, hurt the most.

---

### The Inventory of Absence

She had tried to make a list the night before -- sitting by a fire that barely crackled, scribbling in the margins of a water-damaged field manual.

**Missing:**

- Weapon. (She used to have one. A sword? Or was it an axe? Or a spoon with *intent*?)
- Gold. (Nothing clinked anymore.)
- Compass. (She could follow moss, but it only led her in circles.)
- Sleep. (Elusive.)
- Appetite. (She had eaten, but not tasted.)
- Berylis. (...Blank space. No words.)

She had drawn a small circle next to the last one and shaded it in, darker and darker, until the paper tore.

---

### A Voice That Wasn't

That morning, she'd woken to laughter.

Not her own.

A child's giggle, light and echoing, carried through the mist like a cruel memory. She had turned sharply, hand instinctively reaching for --

-- nothing.

Her fingers grasped air.

Her pack flopped uselessly against her back as she stood. Not even a knife. Not even a broken twig to hold like a threat.

"Hello?" she had called out, her voice thick with unused syllables.

No answer.

Only the trees watching.

Only the silence resettling like dust on a forgotten bookshelf.

---

### A Sign, or a Joke

That afternoon, she found a wooden sign half-sunk in the mud. Hand-painted letters, smudged by weather and time:

> **"Carrion Hill -- 24 miles. Good luck."**

Someone had scrawled beneath it, in a more recent hand:

> "You'll need more than that."

She stared at the words for a long time.

And then she laughed.

A short, bitter bark of sound, ragged and sharp. It bounced off the trees and died quickly -- like it didn't belong here. Like *she* didn't.

She dropped her empty pack beside the sign and sat.

She sat until the sun began to die behind gray clouds, and the air grew colder.

Her shoulders ached -- not from the burden, but from the *lack* of one.

People often said you don't know what you have until it's gone.

Vilga was learning something more painful:

*You don't know how much you carry... until there's nothing left to hold.*

---

### Night Falls on the Hollow

The stars did not come.

The sky above remained a flat sheet of ashen velvet, smothering even the moonlight. In that darkness, Vilga lay down beside her bag, curled up on one side like a comma in the sentence of some other story.

She pulled the flap of her pack over her feet.

It wasn't warm.

But it was... *something*.

A placeholder.

Like she was.

A half-formed thing, waiting for the weight that would make her whole again.

And somewhere, deep in her chest, she missed something she couldn't yet name.

A voice.

A plan.

A laugh beside the fire.

Even just... a spoon with intent.

*To be continued...*

## Volume 2: There Is Spoon

Vilga Deepsack had been walking for what felt like days, but might only have been two hours. Time was slippery in Ustalav, especially when you hadn't packed a timepiece.

Her pack was no longer entirely empty.

Now it held:

- 1 (one) tarnished silver spoon
- 3 (three) withered mushrooms she didn't remember picking
- and a folded note that read: *"You dropped this, idiot."*

The note was unsigned.

And it was written in her own handwriting.

---

### The Ruins of Almost

She came to a crumbled roadside shrine as dusk approached. Ivy choked the stone walls, and a faint scent of rosewater drifted from nowhere in particular. A young woman was already there -- leaning against the altar, arms crossed, skin moon-pale.

She wore a long coat and a bitter expression.

Vilga's breath caught.

The stranger's eyes were a color that should've been illegal -- stormcloud violet rimmed with regret. Her mouth moved like she was about to say something important. Something that would change everything.

Vilga's heart fluttered. Her mouth went dry.

Then her pack gave a sudden clunk.

The spoon had shifted.

Vilga stepped back, panicked. "Sorry! Sorry. Inventory realignment."

The stranger blinked. "What?"

Vilga dropped to her knees and opened her pack. "The spoon. It moved. That means either the mushrooms compressed unevenly, or something else is inside. I need to recalibrate before I'm carrying weird weight distribution into unknown terrain."

The stranger tilted her head. "You... do this often?"

Vilga was elbow-deep in her pack. "Uneven loadout can lead to muscle fatigue and joint misalignment. I'm not risking scoliosis for a vibe."

The woman looked like she wanted to say something else.

Instead, she sighed, turned, and walked away.

Vilga found nothing else in the bag.

But she reorganized it anyway.

---

### The Rehydration Crisis

At camp, Vilga debated rehydrating a mushroom for dinner.

She still had no pot.

But she did have the spoon.

And in the darkness, under a stunted fir tree, she gently carved the words *"Emergency Stew Protocol -- Do Not Initiate Unless Dying"* into its handle with a nail she found sewn into the lining of her coat.

She couldn't remember putting it there.

She also couldn't remember where her waterskin had gone.

She spent an hour searching every pouch in her pack, unfolding and refolding a handkerchief seven times, and checking the same coin pouch at least four times before concluding: it was gone.

---

### Emotional Stakes (Misplaced)

That night, the woman returned in a dream.

She stood over Vilga, holding the missing waterskin in one hand and a broken locket in the other.

"You forgot this," she said.

Vilga reached up.

But the woman pulled away.

"You don't remember me."

"I'm trying," Vilga whispered.

The woman leaned close. Their faces nearly touched.

Vilga's pulse quickened.

Then the woman looked down.

"What kind of ranger doesn't have a proper flint and steel?"

Vilga woke up in a cold sweat and spent the next hour emptying her entire pack to confirm, in fact, she had neither.

She made a checklist.

Then she made a backup checklist.

Then she cried quietly into her cloak.

---

### The Morning After (Inventory Edition)

By dawn, Vilga's pack contained:

- 1 spoon (re-etched for morale)
- 1 mushroom (slightly gnawed)
- 1 backup checklist
- 1 stone she convinced herself was magical, just to justify the weight
- 0 fire-starting tools
- 0 hope
- 1 ache in her chest that might've been a memory... or heartburn.

---

She stood.

She shouldered the weight.

And she kept walking.

To where?

She didn't know.

But she was beginning to suspect that if she ever *did* remember what she'd forgotten --

It wouldn't fit in her pack anyway.

*To be continued...*

## Volume 3: Whispering Waste

The smell hit first.

Vilga paused at the edge of the ruin -- a collapsed chapel half-swallowed by the forest -- and wrinkled her nose. Not quite rot. Not quite mildew.

No, this was the stench of bureaucracy left to die in the sun.

The body lay curled beside the altar. Cloaked in black robes embroidered with faint silver thread -- bones, ravens, and stylized skulls, all distressingly tasteful. Its face was a rictus of surprise and final paperwork left unfinished. The edges of the corpse's sleeves were crisp, unsmudged. Which meant --

"Recent," Vilga muttered. "Or just... very tidy."

She crouched beside the body.

Her pack gave a dull clunk. The spoon shifted again. She sighed.

"Alright, let's do this."

---

### Dissection of the Dead (Logistics Edition)

The cultist's bag was immaculate.

A sleek leather satchel, waterproofed and fire-sealed. Vilga had to admire it. She made a mental note: *Look into getting waxed leather upgrade if we survive next encounter with brain-melting fog.*

Inside, she found:

- 3 (three) **sealed scroll tubes**, embossed with a death's head and the words "Do Not Open Unless Plot-Relevant."
- 1 (one) **tome bound in stitched-together editions of** ***Cheliax for Beginners***. Title in dried blood: *The Tyrant's Tenets: Volume I -- Overthrowing Death with Class.*
- 1 (one) **neatly sorted box of spell reagents**, labeled in tiny bone charms. Categories included:
    - *"Hair -- Humanoid (Ash Blonde)"*
    - *"Ash -- Saint-Class"*
    - *"Whale Bone -- Ritual Grade"*
    - *"Confetti -- Black, Cursed"*
- 12 (twelve) **Whispering Way sticky notes**, each inscribed with ominous motivational phrases:
    - "Smile! Your death serves a greater evil!"
    - "Organize your components before judgment day."
    - "Even liches respect tidy workspaces."

Vilga read them all.

Twice.

Then she found the **notebook**.

---

### The Personal Touch of Evil

The cover was leather. Embossed. Gold-accented. Custom engraved.

> "Property of Brother Elrik of the Ninth Breath
> Whispering Way, Northeast Chapter (Caliphas Cell)
> If found, return to Gallowspire. Reward: Eternal Torment."

Inside, the entries were handwritten in careful, fussy calligraphy. To-do lists. Passive-aggressive notes to cellmates. Several increasingly desperate diary entries about missing cauldrons, unwashed robes, and how "*the new guy keeps borrowing my alchemical glue and I KNOW he's using it on the undead rodents again.*"

On page 37, she found this:

> "Inventory -- travel set:
>
> - 1x sacrificial dagger (blood slot: unused)
> - 2x Whispering Way pens (enchanted ink, necrotic)
> - 1x map to Carrion Hill (folded wrong by accident, burned later)
> - 1x spare robe
> - 4x packets soul-dried rations (tastes like guilt)
> - 1x letter from Mom (burned for morale)"

Vilga gently closed the book.

And then -- because she knew she shouldn't -- she opened the satchel's **side pouch**.

---

### Romance, Ruined Again

Inside was:

- A **sealed letter**, addressed in elegant looping hand:

    > *To Tarvis,*
    > *I hope the performance goes well. Don't over-enunciate the word "lich" this time -- it made everyone uncomfortable. Love, Elrik.*

- And...

- A pressed flower. A single **black tulip**, lovingly flattened between a note that read:
    *"Still remember Ascanor. The moonlight. The cold wine. The stolen map. Miss you."*

Vilga stared at it.

The moment was fragile. Real. Even a little sad.

Then her own pack gave a sudden creak.

The spoon had fallen into the sticky notes.

Again.

She cursed softly and began reorganizing both bags.

---

### Ghosts in the Margins

It was nearly sundown when the whispering began.

Soft. Subtle. Like guilt being stirred with a ladle.

Words spilled from the dead cultist's gear -- not spoken aloud, but *implied* by the shifting wind and the unblinking crows.

> *"Bring it to Carrion Hill."*
> *"The Fuckboi must not be allowed to rise."*
> *"Use the letterhead."*

Vilga blinked.

One of the Whispering Way pens had rolled from the cultist's bag into her own.

The ink shimmered in the growing dark -- greenish-black, like spoiled wine.

Her fingers hovered over it.

It was probably cursed.

But it would *look so good* on her list templates.

She picked it up.

---

### Final Inventory

She walked out of the ruin with:

- 1 fancy cult pen
- 1 journal full of passive-aggressive necromancer drama
- 1 soulbound tulip
- 1 letter possibly useful for blackmail
- 4 sticky notes labeled "DO NOT COMPOST"

And one more burden:

**She was starting to remember Tarvis.**

Not clearly.

Not kindly.

But enough.

*To be continued...*

## Volume 4: The Weight of Wealth

The village was barely more than a bend in the river and a scattering of roofs, but to Vilga, it might as well have been Absalom itself.

**A general store.**
An actual, functioning, legally ambiguous, poorly lit general store.

She didn't cry when she saw it.

But her pack groaned in relief.

---

### Commerce, Blessed Be Thy Overlooked Shelves

The proprietor was a man with no nose and too many opinions. His name was either **Grumm** or **"Get on with it"**, depending on who you asked.

He peered into Vilga's open satchel with the kind of reverence reserved for relics and suspicious tax write-offs.

"You got Whispering Way gear," he muttered. "And organized. That's rare."

She nodded, unsmiling. "He had good handwriting."

Grumm lifted a pen and whistled. "Necro-ink. Animated calligraphy. You got a permit for this?"

She stared at him.

He slid it back across the counter. "Never mind."

---

### The Great Liquidation

She sold:

- 1 evil tome (with annotations)
- 1 sticky note set (minus the "YOU GOT THIS, CULTIST" page, which she kept)
- 1 vial labeled "Wight Saliva -- artisanal"
- 1 pressed tulip (emotional value: priceless; resale value: shockingly high)
- 1 haunted spoon (no, not *her* spoon -- the other one, whispering "regret" on loop)

Grumm paid her **74 gold** and **3 silver**.

Vilga blinked. "That can't be right."

He shrugged. "Collectors pay through the teeth for cult crap. You didn't even haggle. Want it in coin or store credit?"

She hesitated.

She should take the coin.

She really, *really* should take the coin.

---

She took the store credit.

---

### Meanwhile, in the Woven Goods Aisle...

An hour later, Vilga stood at the checkout, arms full of:

- 1 collapsible alchemy set (she didn't *do* alchemy)
- 3 cookbooks, including *Eating Well in the Shudderwood* and *101 Things To Do With Root Vegetables You Hate*
- 1 spool of grave-silk rope ("glows slightly, might be sentient")
- 12 vials of "Emergency Mood Enhancers" (dried flowers and speculative hope)
- 1 book of romance-themed survival haikus
- 1 t-shirt that said *I Raised the Whispering Tyrant and All I Got Was This Stupid Curse*

Her new pack -- bought because the old one had "trauma saturation" -- was already at capacity.

She was trying to wedge in the rope when someone behind her said:

"You realize you don't need *any* of that, right?"

---

### Enter: Berylis Midload

She turned.

And *there* she was.

Leather armor. Practical braid. Judgy eyebrows. One hand on the hilt of a sword that had definitely seen more use than Vilga's spoon. A single backpack on her shoulders, looking appropriately burdened and *suspiciously balanced*.

Vilga's breath caught in her throat for half a second.

Then she scoffed. "I *need* the grave rope."

"You don't even *climb* things."

"It's for emergencies."

Berylis snorted. "And the mood flowers?"

"I'm working through some things."

A pause.

Then, awkwardly: "I like your spoon."

Vilga blinked. "Thank you. I, uh. Polish it."

Another pause. Longer this time. The grave rope coiled ominously at their feet.

---

### The Beginning of the Burden

They shared a table at the village's only inn. Vilga sipped something labeled "liquor," Berylis drank water, and their packs leaned together under the bench -- groaning slightly under the combined pressure of too many choices.

"So," Berylis said. "You heading to Carrion Hill?"

Vilga nodded.

"Tracking the necromancer?"

Another nod.

"Good. You're not going to make it with that loadout."

Vilga scowled. "It's curated."

"It's insane."

A long silence.

Then Berylis slid a hand-drawn flowchart across the table.

> *Efficient Packing for Encounters: A Decision Tree.*

Vilga looked up, stunned.

Berylis smirked.

"We start reorganizing in the morning."

---

### Final Inventory

**Vilga's Pack** now contained:

- 1 grave rope (slightly alive)
- 3 cookbooks
- 1 spoon (still THE spoon)
- 1 unwanted emotional vulnerability
- 1 unfamiliar sensation labeled "companionship"

She couldn't explain it.

But her back didn't ache as much when Berylis was walking beside her.

She was still overburdened.

But not *alone*.

*To be continued...*

## Volume 5: Pack to Pack

The forest path was narrower now.

One of those winding, mud-choked trails that existed more out of local superstition than infrastructure. Thorned undergrowth clawed at their cloaks, roots jutted up like tripwires. Carrion birds circled overhead, cawing in ways that sounded judgmental.

Still, the two of them walked on.

Side by side.

Burdened.

And -- for now -- blissfully unaware that the real danger wasn't wolves, or undead, or arcane traps.

It was **each other's loadout methodology**.

---

### Honeymoon Phase of the Inventory Partnership

For the first day, it was almost romantic.

Berylis carried tools. Sharp-edged things. Lockpicks. Daggers. A single, well-oiled crossbow in a custom leather holster.

Vilga carried miscellanea. Maps. Ropes. Dried mango. A brass spyglass she'd never used. The grave rope, now sulking in its own pouch.

They admired each other in quiet moments:

- Vilga watched Berylis label her pouches by encounter type.
- Berylis noted how Vilga's spoon pouch had **dedicated cushioning** and was always within reach.

"You keep your rations *separate* from your emergency rations," Berylis said over breakfast, clearly impressed.

"Redundancy is preparedness," Vilga replied.

They shared a look.

The birds stopped cawing.

The spoon glinted.

It was good.

For a time.

---

### The Bridge of Mild Disagreements

It started at the bridge.

Or what *used* to be a bridge -- now a half-collapsed nightmare of splintered timbers and ominous fog. They stood on opposite banks, eyeing the remains.

"We'll have to cross one at a time," Berylis said.

"No problem," Vilga said, tightening her straps.

Berylis paused. "Wait. You're wearing your alchemy set **now**?"

"Yes."

"Why?"

"In case there's a surprise need for *solvent*."

"It's a 50-foot drop."

"And acid *melts* rope traps."

"There is no rope trap!"

Vilga narrowed her eyes. "Yet."

---

### Packing Philosophies Diverge

Once across, they camped under a canopy of dying willows. The rain started lightly. Just enough to raise tensions.

They unpacked together for the first time.

Vilga spread out her items by **aesthetic categories**: "glowing," "shiny," and "emotionally significant."

Berylis unpacked by **utility brackets**: "emergency," "combat," "support," and "miscellaneous judgment calls."

They both reached for the central cooking pot at the same time.

It was *not* there.

"Wait," Vilga said, blinking. "I thought *you* were carrying it."

Berylis froze. "You said you were reallocating nonessentials to me."

Vilga frowned. "You called soup a *luxury*."

"It *is*."

"You absolute monster."

---

### Passive-Aggressive Inventorying

The argument didn't escalate.

It **settled**.

Like a silt of disappointment.

The rest of the evening was silent, save for the wet sound of jerky being gnawed without joy.

At one point, Berylis muttered, "Why do you carry twelve candles?"

"In case I need to stage a ritual or set a mood," Vilga snapped. "Why do you carry **four** signal whistles?"

"I lose things, okay?"

A long silence.

Then, quietly:

"I liked it better when we were admiring each other's differences," Berylis said.

Vilga nodded.

"I liked that too."

---

### Resolution, Softly Folded

In the morning, Berylis found her bedroll had been repacked for her.

It was snug. Better weighted. The knots in her pack no longer pulled at her shoulders.

Vilga looked away as she adjusted her own gear. "I borrowed your balance chart."

Berylis grunted.

Then gently set a small, unmarked pouch beside Vilga.

Vilga opened it.

Inside: the missing cooking pot.

And two collapsible spoons.

---

### Final Inventory

- Berylis: Slightly less annoyed. Slightly more sentimental.
- Vilga: Emotionally vulnerable. Smelling faintly of campfire and compromise.
- Shared: 1 pot. 2 spoons. 0 bridges burned.

They walked on.

Still overburdened.

Still unspoken.

But now -- **interwoven**.

## Volume 6: Disorder Most Foul

The wind carried the smell of mold and iron.

Berylis crouched low, her fingers pressed to a patch of torn earth. "Wagon tracks," she muttered. "Deep. Overloaded. Swerving erratically."

Vilga knelt beside her. "Like someone packing for a funeral... but also a beach vacation."

They shared a grim look.

**Something had been here.**
And judging by the half-buried wheel jutting from the nearby hill, it hadn't left in good condition.

---

### The Trove of Untold Stupidity

The wagon was smashed sideways into a ditch, covered in moss and regret. Its canopy hung in shredded curtains. Dozens -- **dozens** -- of wooden crates, leather satchels, and lockboxes had spilled out, forming a landscape of poorly restrained greed.

At first, it looked like treasure.

Then they opened the first chest.

And beheld the horror within.

---

### Inventory Sin: The Reckoning

Inside the chest:

- 1 (one) moldy sandwich (unwrapped)
- 2 (two) vials labeled "DRINK ME PROBABLY"
- 17 (seventeen) gold coins wrapped individually in socks
- A bundle of arrows glued together into one giant "arrow bouquet"

Berylis recoiled. "What the hell am I looking at?"

Vilga picked up a scroll that had been folded *triangularly*. "They folded magic. They **folded magic**."

They moved to the next crate. And the next. And it kept getting worse.

---

**Offenses included:**

- Gems packed in *wet cloth*
- Crossbow bolts stored **tip-up** in a wine bottle
- Two wands taped together with string labeled "DOUBLE EFFECT???"
- A spellbook inside a *pot of stew* (onions still present)
- Sticky notes with battle plans, all in *Wingdings*

---

Vilga screamed into the sky.

Berylis kicked over a bucket labeled "Important Dust."

And still they searched. Compelled. Outraged. United.

---

### The Culprits Revealed

They found the bodies half-buried under a collapsed chest labeled **"Definitely Not Traps"**.

Skeletons in mismatched armor. Rusted badges reading *"The Rummaging Jackals -- Loot First, Ask Later"*.

Bandits. Clearly.

And -- based on the fact that one of them was still chewing on a holy symbol -- they'd angered something unholy and died mid-loot.

Vilga held up a bag of coins that also contained:

- Two teeth
- A lizard skull
- Half a spell scroll about "Optimizing Saddlebags"

"This is violence," she said.

Berylis nodded. "They died doing what they loved -- hoarding like idiots."

---

### Fury in Symmetry

They began sorting.

Together.

- Weapons in a pile (mostly salvaged)
- Spell components in a heap (after rescuing them from a *soggy jerky pouch*)
- Coins wiped down and dried
- Unreadable scrolls stacked for future "ritual burning, possibly romantic"

They didn't speak much.

But they moved in sync.

By nightfall, they'd repacked what was usable and burned what made them cry.

Vilga looked up, sweat on her brow, soot on her cheek. "You know..."

Berylis glanced at her.

"This is the most emotionally intimate thing I've ever done."

Berylis wiped her hands on her pants. "Same."

They stared at each other.

The moment stretched.

And then --

Vilga sneezed directly into her spell pouch.

"Godsdammit," she muttered, blowing glitter.

Berylis threw a damp handkerchief at her face.

---

### Final Inventory

- 1 newly acquired bag of semi-usable treasure
- 1 joint journal of "Organizational Crimes Witnessed"
- 2 pairs of gloves labeled "For Sorting Only"
- 1 deepening partnership rooted in **shared, righteous fury**

They left the wreck behind.

Shoulders sore.

Hearts... ever so slightly less encumbered.

## Volume 7: Encumbered Hearts

The moon hung bloated and spectral over the craggy hills of Ustalav, bathing the uneven path in silver mist. The land smelled of damp earth, cold stone, and distant rot -- the perfect setting for a vampire hunt.

But Vilga Deepsack and Berylis Midload had bigger problems.

They had just survived an encounter with the single worst loot organization either had ever witnessed -- bandits who stored flammable oils next to heatstones and kept their scrolls rolled around pickles. Their tolerance for disorder was gone. Their backs hurt. And one of them -- though neither would say who -- was still carrying a vial labeled *"probably cursed, IDK."*

"We should make camp soon," Vilga panted, adjusting the thick leather straps digging into her shoulders. "I can't feel my spine."

She had attempted to reweight her new collapsible tent kit after lunch, but Berylis had argued her calculations were "based on emotional packing, not physics."

"You're exaggerating," Berylis scoffed, though her own pack swayed dangerously under its overstuffed burden. Her toned arms trembled as she tried to readjust its weight without toppling over.

She had insisted on carrying the vampire decoy dummy. "It's thematic," she'd said. "We're hunting vampires." It had not helped morale.

A metallic clang rang through the valley as one of her pouches burst open, spilling silvered stakes onto the muddy path.

Vilga sighed. "That's the third time this hour."

Berylis exhaled sharply, watching as a vial of holy water rolled toward the edge of a steep incline. "Fucking hells." She lunged for it, but the added momentum of her pack tipped her forward, and she barely caught herself before toppling off the path.

Vilga grabbed her by the belt, yanking her back with a grunt. "This is getting out of hand. When we started this job, you had one crossbow and a dagger. Now you're carrying --" She reached over and flipped open a bulging side pouch. "Three wooden mallets? Berylis, what in the unholy name of Zon-Kuthon are you doing with three?"

"You told me yourself -- redundancy is preparedness," Berylis shot back. "You carry three cookbooks."

Vilga ran a gloved hand down her face. "And the anvil?"

Berylis's jaw set stubbornly. "You never know when you need an anvil."

They stood in silence, the weight of their absurdly overstocked packs pressing them deeper into the mud.

The sexual tension, like the weight of their gear, was unbearable.

Vilga's piercing amber eyes flicked to Berylis's full lips, slightly parted as she caught her breath. The huntress was rugged perfection -- dark hair tied back in a practical, no-nonsense braid, her leather armor stretched tight over defined curves. Gods, if only she weren't carrying fifty pounds of alchemical components.

Berylis, meanwhile, was keenly aware of Vilga's roughened fingers gripping her belt, the heat of her body radiating through layers of gear. She wanted nothing more than to throw this woman down into the cold grass and press against her until dawn -- but every time she tried to get close, their belts caught on each other, or a stray grappling hook jutted out at an unfortunate angle.

Vilga exhaled sharply. "Let's -- let's just take a break. Get some of this weight off."

Berylis hesitated, then nodded. They both dropped their packs at the same time. The impact shook the ground, sending a family of startled bats screeching from a nearby copse of trees.

For a long moment, they just stood there, rolling sore shoulders, stretching stiffened muscles.

Then Vilga turned, eyes smoldering.

"We've been at this for weeks," she murmured, stepping closer. "I can't tell if my back is sore from the weight... or from the way you look at me when you think I'm not watching."

Berylis's lips curled into a smirk. "What, like this?"

She took a step forward, tilting her head, gaze raking over Vilga's battle-worn form. Their bodies were inches apart now, free from the bulk of their gear, the heat between them finally unencumbered.

Vilga's breath hitched. "Exactly like that."

Then, from behind them, their packs gave an ominous creak.

The straps, weakened by hours of strain, snapped -- and a landslide of silvered stakes, vials, chains, mallets, and gods-know-what-else erupted onto the road, sweeping their feet out from under them.

They went down hard.

Berylis landed first, flat on her back, and Vilga tumbled down on top of her, face inches from her companion's. Their bodies pressed together, the only thing separating them being a loose coil of consecrated rope that had somehow wedged itself between their stomachs.

The same rope they had argued about for ten minutes that morning. Vilga wanted it coiled. Berylis had looped it. Neither had won.

For a moment, they just lay there.

Berylis groaned. "Tell me the Fuckboi of Carrion Hill is worth this."

"He's not," Vilga said. "But I want to hit him with a reorganized inventory list so hard it knocks the smug off his soul."

Berylis winced. "Fair."

Vilga, still half-straddling her, smirked. "I'd rather talk about something else."

Berylis arched a brow. "Yeah?"

"Yeah." Vilga leaned in, fingers trailing along Berylis's jaw. "Like how we can reduce our total carrying weight by twenty percent if we consolidate duplicate items."

"You're still carrying two jars of vampire repellent lotion," Berylis muttered.

"They stack emotionally," Vilga replied.

Berylis groaned again, this time in pure frustration. "Just kiss me, you overprepared bastard."

Vilga grinned. "Not until we reorganize our packs."

## Volume 8: Damn the Dam of the Damned

The next morning, with backs aching and dignity in shambles, Vilga Deepsack and Berylis Midload set out once more. Their absurdly overstocked packs were now *slightly* -- and only *technically* -- more organized. The tension from the previous night lingered in the air, thick as the mist that curled along the swampy banks of the river.

They had been tracking their bounty -- the Fuckboi of Carrion Hill, a necromancer with a smug face and the evasiveness of a greased ferret -- for nearly a week now. Every time they closed in, he slipped away, leaving behind a trail of unpaid tavern tabs, cryptic flirtation, and an increasing number of animated corpses doing chores.

It was one such abomination that now blocked their path.

A dam.

A dam made of bloated, rotting **beavers**, their half-decomposed bodies wedged between waterlogged logs and tangled reeds. Their empty, blackened eyes stared outward like judgmental marbles. One twitched.

Berylis sighed heavily. "I don't know what's worse -- the fact that I can *see* them breathing, or the fact that I can *hear* them breathing."

Vilga dragged a gloved hand down her face. "Undead beavers. Of course. Because why wouldn't that smirking little spellhole reanimate aquatic rodents into civil engineers?"

The dam pulsed.

The beavers -- silent no longer -- began to chatter.

Then, with a hideous, gurgling squeal, the entire dam burst apart as the zombified beavers launched themselves at the duo in a grotesque, synchronized wave of gnashing teeth and clawing, webbed paws.

---

### The Tactical Disaster of the Century

Vilga and Berylis reacted as trained hunters should -- blades flashing, crossbows firing, spells slicing the fog like lightning through soup.

It was going well.

Until they noticed what the beavers were targeting.

Not their throats.

Not their legs.

**Their weapons.**

The undead rodents had a singular mission: disarm, disable, and disorganize.

One beaver latched onto Vilga's longsword and *ripped it clean away* with the force of petty vengeance.

Another sank its filthy teeth into Berylis's crossbow, dragging it into the writhing tide of chittering horror.

A third beaver locked eyes with Berylis as it consumed an entire pearl of power like a walnut.

**Pop. Gone.**

They were now unarmed.

And the beavers were *not* done.

Berylis vaulted onto a nearby stump, reaching for her backup dagger -- only for a particularly vengeful zom-beaver to climb after her, gnawing through the sheath before she could even draw.

Vilga's eyes darted, calculating.

They had no swords.
No crossbows.
No magic.

But they did have something else.

Something heavier.

Something **stupider**.

---

### The Sound of a Hundred-Pound Satchel Smashing a Beaver to Death

Vilga tore her pack from her shoulders with a grunt and swung it like a warhammer into the nearest zom-beaver.

The creature was flattened instantly, its gelatinous remains splattering across the mud.

Berylis paused mid-curse.

"Gods," she whispered. "We're stupid."

With a feral grin, she whipped her backpack overhead and brought it down like a guillotine onto another beaver, crushing it beneath fifty pounds of over-preparedness and unresolved emotional tension.

The battlefield shifted.

The beavers had made a fatal mistake.

They had taken the duo's blades.

But they had left them their **greatest strength**:

- Backup cookware
- Three cookbooks
- A pouch of haunted silver stakes
- A **slightly sentient rope**
- And centuries of repressed lesbian rage

Vilga pivoted and swung again, pulverizing three more zom-beavers in a single, sweeping arc.

Berylis executed a brutal overhead smash, pinning two creatures under her medical kit and backup frying pan.

Another beaver lunged -- only to be intercepted mid-air by a flying bedroll and a jar of potion-thickened jam.

A fourth was struck so hard it ricocheted off a tree and impaled itself on a silvered stake it had tried to steal.

It was carnage.

Soggy, mud-soaked, **undead-rodent-smashing carnage**.

And within minutes, the beavers were no more.

---

### Aftermath & A Lesson in Tactical Overpacking

Vilga stood panting, sweat mixing with mud, shoulders sore from swinging eighty pounds of travel trauma like a club.

Berylis wiped her face with the edge of her cloak, which now smelled permanently of beaver and regret.

The dam had collapsed completely. The remains of undead beavers floated downstream, a warning to any necromancer foolish enough to reanimate wildlife without proper containment procedures.

"I am never," Berylis wheezed, "ever... getting rid of my backup mallets."

Vilga nudged a twitching corpse with her boot. "We should report this to the Pathfinder Society. Or the druids. Or whatever sad bastard handles river infrastructure."

Berylis sighed. "Yeah. Right after we find that necromantic little shit and beat him senseless."

With aching muscles and a renewed sense of purpose, they hoisted their indestructible, overstuffed packs once more.

They had no clean socks.

They had no surviving spell components.

But they had each other.

And that was heavier than either of them expected.

## Volume 9: Carry-On Hell

The road to Carrion Hill was less a road and more a series of escalating geological threats. Pitted stone. Ankle-deep sludge. The occasional sinkhole that whispered rude things in Aklo. Still, the duo trudged forward -- burdened, blistered, and bilious from the previous day's buffet of undead pestilence.

They didn't speak much.

But their packs creaked in solidarity.

---

As they crested a fog-choked ridge, the city came into view.

**Carrion Hill.**

The Crows, its twisted towers, loomed like broken fingers reaching from a mass grave. Every rooftop sagged under the weight of secrets. Every alley reeked of mildew, regret, and tax fraud. A murder of crows wheeled overhead, cawing in what might have been prophecy -- or profanity.

Vilga scowled at the skyline. "Gods, I hate this place."

Berylis spat into the mud. "That's the spirit."

---

They passed through the east gate without incident -- if one discounted the guard's *uncontrollable laughter* at the sight of their gear, still crusted in beaver guts, root rot, and deeply repressed attraction.

Inside, the streets bustled in that uniquely Ustalavic way: hurried, haunted, and **desperately pretending everything was normal**.

And business -- gods help them -- they had.

---

They made straight for the Witch's Market in the Tangles district, weaving through crooked stalls and even crookeder merchants hawking everything from curse-proof socks to responsibly sourced ghoul teeth.

"Oi!" barked a vendor with three eyepatches and no shirt. "You two look like women of refined vengeance. Fancy a discount on soul jars? No leaks this batch!"

Vilga didn't break stride. "Do I look like I want *another* jar?"

They reached a lopsided tent stitched from crow feathers and witchfloss. A diviner's hut, if one were feeling generous.

Before they could knock, the flap lifted.

Inside, a head turned.

Not a body. Just a **head**. Severed. Perched on a silver plate. Cheerful.

"Ladies," it said in a voice like velvet dragged over thornbushes. "You look like you've been through a beaver apocalypse."

Vilga gestured at the head, glancing sideways at Berylis. "I know how much you love talking to heads..."

Berylis crossed her arms. "We're looking for the Fuckboi of Carrion Hill. Necromancer. Smug. Smells like old wine and unresolved drama."

"Oh yes," purred the head. "You mean Tarvis. He was here. Paid for six foresights, two minor curses, and a fortune cookie. Then he left a bad review and ran off."

Vilga narrowed her eyes. "Where did he go?"

A long pause.

Then, from a shadowed corner of the tent, a hand emerged, holding a crumpled flyer.

It read:

> **The Fuckboi Rises Again!**
> *One Night Only!*
> *Live Undead Performance at The Drowned Harlot Tavern.*
> *Midnight. No clerics allowed.*

Berylis rubbed her temple. "We should've just followed the smell of bad decisions."

---

### Interlude: The Drowned Harlot

If you've never been to **The Drowned Harlot**, imagine a haunted tavern designed by a hungover banshee and a drunk architect, then set partially on fire and rebuilt using stolen headstones and spite.

Its clientele were loyal. Its mead was suspect. Its bouncer was a **reanimated owlbear** in a feathered boa.

The tavern throbbed with unnatural light and off-key necro-pop. A skeletal bard was performing a tambourine-forward remix of *Grave Danger (Thuvia Nights Edit)*.

And there he was.

Center stage.

**Tarvis. The Fuckboi.**

Shirt unbuttoned to the navel. Smugness set to maximum. Surrounded by a troupe of synchronized ghoul dancers dressed like tax collectors.

"WHO'S READY TO RAISE THE DEAD!" he shouted.

The crowd cheered.

Vilga stared in silence. "I'm going to kill him."

Berylis adjusted the straps on her pack. "With what? Your charisma?"

"No. With luggage-based violence."

---

### Curtain Call of the Cursed

Their plan was not elegant.

It involved:

- Sneaking past the owlbear by bribing it with sausages.
- Climbing onto the rafters above the stage.
- Coordinating a synchronized pack-drop assault.

They succeeded at two out of three.

The owlbear took the sausages and, uninvited, came with them.

The rafters creaked like a guilty conscience in a confessional booth.

Below, Tarvis launched into a power ballad about liches and love lost in Thuvia.

Vilga and Berylis locked eyes.

And then they dropped their packs.

**Direct hit.**

Tarvis was flattened beneath forty pounds of cookware, blessed rope, loose stakes, and at least one pocket of regret granola.

The crowd **applauded**.

Someone shouted, "Encore!"

---

Berylis dropped down, dagger drawn. "If you animate *one more beaver*, I swear --"

Tarvis groaned, bleeding glitter. "It was performance art!"

Vilga kicked him in the ribs. "Your art is about to be *posthumous*."

---

### Aftermath & The Cost of Luggage

They hauled him out, bound in enchanted zip-ties.

The owlbear got a free drink.

The skeletal bard composed a dirge in their honor:
*"Burdened, but Unbroken."*

As they dragged Tarvis through Carrion Hill's twisting alleys -- bruised, humiliated, and still humming his own chorus -- Vilga muttered:

"Next time... let's just hunt smugglers."

Berylis laughed. "And give up the glamorous world of necromantic showbiz? Never."

Their packs clanked.

Their boots squelched.

Their shoulders throbbed.

But something between them had settled -- like a well-balanced satchel after a long walk.

They vanished into the mist, dragging justice, gear, and one dramatic dumbass behind them.

Carrion Hill sighed.

And deep in the sewers, a zombified beaver twitched... ominously.
