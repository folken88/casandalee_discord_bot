## Summary

The Justice Gorls attend the funeral of Professor Vellesca Martin at the Church of Abadar, one of the finest cathedrals in Caliphas. The party receives a handwritten invitation with a charcoal drawing of the professor, signed by 14 grad students. The eulogy describes Professor Martin as a heroic adventurer, generous philanthropist who donated 75% of her salary to charity, and beloved teacher. The party is publicly honored as the heroes who rescued the professor from the Skanktown Killers. Several party members deliver emotional (and awkward) eulogies at the funeral. Slass also brings word of a new job for next week.

## Key Events

- Slass Venton ("Sassy") delivers a letter to the party at their hideout, addressed to the mercenaries who saved the professor. He mentions having a new job for them next week.
- Taylor opens the letter: it is a handwritten invitation to the celebration of life for one of Caliphas's most respected researchers, with reserved seating in the first three rows at the Church of Abadar.
- The invitation includes an original charcoal drawing of the professor, signed by 14 grad students.
- The party discusses funeral attire -- no armor, no weapons. Some conceal daggers in boots, and one character brings a fighting fan (which passes as a non-weapon). Lou's quarterstaff is justified as a walking aid.
- The eulogy at the Church of Abadar reveals Professor Vellesca Martin's heroism: she rescued a family of four from a burning building, helped police catch the Bone Bandit of Bend Avenue with a slide tackle, and donated 75% of her salary to local charities.
- The party is publicly acknowledged as the heroes who saved Professor Martin from the Skanktown Killers (a gang whose leader Agu beheaded, earning the title "Queen of Skanktown").
- The eulogy states the heroes traveled with the professor, rescued her, and fought by her side until all students were safely returned.

- Another character gives an awkwardly honest speech about how they probably should not be there, ending simply with "we are here" -- which the grad students enthusiastically echo.
- A third character delivers the most polished eulogy, saying everyone should be honored to have lived in the same time as Professor Martin.
- The provost of the university is in attendance.
- The episode title comes from a grad student's tearful remark: "My heart is pass-fail."
