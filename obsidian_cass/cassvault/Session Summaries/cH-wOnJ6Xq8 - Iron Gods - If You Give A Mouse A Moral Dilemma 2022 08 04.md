## Summary

# Iron Gods Campaign Journal: "If You Give A Mouse A Moral Dilemma"

The party began this session at a ruin site, strategizing their approach to the Dominion threat. After discussing travel plans and methods for charging vehicle capacitors—including a creative proposal for a lightning capture device—the group moved to engage three floating, crab-like Dominion creatures equipped with blaster pistols near a meat pile. The party successfully defeated these metallic enemies, securing their corpses as proof of the kill for their ongoing deal with Binox. Following this victory, the party recovered the Patty Wagon from an underwater location by enlarging themselves to ten feet tall and using rope to retrieve the submerged vehicle.

As the party traveled eastward toward Binocs in their recovered Prime Mobile, they made camp in the forest and noticed a significant change: with the death of the oppressive ghost that had haunted the area, wildlife had returned. Crickets, owls, and normal forest sounds filled the night once more. However, their respite was short-lived when Izuma warned them that a female Binoxian robot was positioned approximately fifty feet from their camp, still monitoring their activities. The party completed three uneventful watch rotations before a startling discovery disrupted their rest.

Luna, attempting to investigate a mysterious backpack belonging to Tokala, transformed into a mouse and infiltrated the pack to dispel any active magic. She discovered the container was a magically-forged technological device with residual magical aura but no active spells. When discovered, Luna barely escaped as Tokala attempted to kill the mouse by smashing the backpack. Later, another party member used a warp metal spell to break open a sealed magical container, revealing its contents: a sentient brain in a jar—a creature from the Technic League that Tokala had been secretly carrying.

This discovery sparked intense moral debate within the party. Tokala argued the brain should be destroyed, just as they had destroyed an airship previously. Other party members countered that keeping the creature alive while imprisoned was cruel and equivalent to slavery. The argument escalated when Tokala deliberately pressed buttons on the brain device while maintaining eye contact with another party member, causing it to scream and wake the camp. When confronted, Tokala attempted to convince the party he was merely trying to communicate with the brain to bridge understanding, though his bluff checks (ranging from 12 to 24) left many unconvinced. The party remained divided on whether to extract information from the suffering creature or mercifully disconnect it, leaving this moral dilemma unresolved as the session concluded.
